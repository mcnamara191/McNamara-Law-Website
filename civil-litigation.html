Holding businesses accountable

  • Repair-shop disputes, including overcharges, bad work, and held vehicles
  • Contractor & service disputes over unfinished or defective work
  • Recovery of personal property wrongfully kept by someone else

How a Case Moves

From demand letter to resolution

Many disputes resolve before a lawsuit is ever filed. When they don't, we are ready.

i

Case evaluation

We review your documents, identify every legal claim, and give you an honest view of value, cost, and risk.

ii

Demand & negotiation

A well-supported demand letter often brings the other side to the table, and some statutes require pre-suit notice.

iii

Filing suit

If needed, we file in the proper Florida court and serve the defendant.

iv

Discovery & mediation

Both sides exchange documents and testimony. Most civil cases are sent to mediation before trial.

v

Resolution or trial

Settlement on fair terms, or a judgment from the court, followed by collection if necessary.

Generally, five years for a written contract and four years for an oral contract, measured from when the breach occurred. Other claims have different deadlines; FDUTPA claims, for example, are generally subject to a four-year limitation period. Deadlines can be affected by the facts, so don't wait.

Sometimes. Florida generally follows the rule that each side pays its own fees unless a contract or statute provides otherwise. Many contracts include a prevailing-party fee clause, and statutes like FDUTPA and the Motor Vehicle Repair Act allow courts to award fees. Fee provisions can cut both ways, which is part of evaluating any case.

In Florida, small claims handles disputes up to $8,000, county court handles claims up to $50,000, and circuit court handles larger claims, excluding interest, costs, and fees. The right court affects the procedure, timeline, and strategy.
