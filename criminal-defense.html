When to Call

Representation before charges are filed and after.

Many people wait until they're formally charged to hire a lawyer. In Florida, the State Attorney's Office decides whether to file charges, and which charges to file, after reviewing the police investigation. The earlier you have counsel, the more opportunities there are to shape that decision.

Before Charges

Pre-filing & investigation representation

If a detective has called you, police want to "get your side of the story," you've received a target letter, or you were arrested and are waiting for a filing decision, now is the time to act.

  • We handle communication with law enforcement so you don't make statements that become evidence against you
  • We present mitigating information to the prosecutor before a charging decision is made
  • We advocate for reduced charges, diversion, or no charges where the facts support it
  • We protect your rights during searches, interviews, and requests for evidence
After Charges

Defending a filed criminal case

Once charges are filed, your case moves on a court schedule with real deadlines. We step in at any stage and build the defense from the evidence up.

  • Bond & release conditions, including no-contact orders
  • Arraignment & discovery, including police reports, video, and witness statements
  • Motions to suppress or dismiss when evidence was obtained unlawfully
  • Negotiation toward reduced charges, diversion, or a withhold of adjudication
  • Trial when the right answer is to make the State prove its case
Criminal Defense Services

Florida criminal matters we handle

From traffic-related misdemeanors to serious felonies, every charge deserves a careful defense.

Misdemeanor

Misdemeanor Defense

A misdemeanor can still mean jail, probation, fines, and a permanent record. We fight to keep your record clean through dismissal, diversion, or a withhold of adjudication where possible.

Felony

Felony Defense

Felony convictions carry prison exposure and lifelong consequences for employment, housing, firearms, and immigration status. We prepare every felony case thoroughly from the first day.

Fla. Stat. § 316.193

DUI

A DUI arrest creates two cases: the criminal charge and an administrative license suspension. You generally have only 10 days from the arrest to request a review of the suspension.

Fla. Stat. § 322.34

Driving While License Suspended / Revoked

Knowingly driving while suspended is a crime, and penalties increase with each offense; a third conviction can be a felony. Many suspensions can be resolved, which can change the outcome of the case.

Battery · DV

Battery & Domestic Violence

Domestic violence arrests move fast and often come with no-contact orders and injunctions affecting your home and children. The State, not the alleged victim, decides whether a case proceeds.

Theft · Property

Theft & Property Crimes

Retail theft, grand theft, burglary, criminal mischief, and dealing in stolen property. Value thresholds and prior history can turn a misdemeanor into a felony.

Fla. Stat. Ch. 893

Drug Charges

Possession, possession with intent, paraphernalia, and trafficking charges. Search-and-seizure issues are often central, and diversion or drug court may be available.

Probation

Violation of Probation

A violation is decided by a judge, not a jury, under a lower standard of proof, and the judge can impose up to the maximum sentence for the original offense. Fast, prepared representation matters.

Fla. Stat. § 943.0585 · .059

Sealing & Expungement

Eligible records can be sealed or expunged so they no longer appear on most background checks. Eligibility rules are strict and generally allow one sealing or expungement in a lifetime.

How a Case Moves

The stages of a Florida criminal case

Knowing what comes next takes some of the fear out of the process. We'll guide you through each stage.

i

Investigation or arrest

Police investigate, make an arrest, or issue a notice to appear. Pre-filing representation can begin here.

ii

First appearance & bond

After an arrest, a judge reviews probable cause and sets bond and release conditions.

iii

Filing & arraignment

The State Attorney decides what to file. At arraignment, a plea of not guilty is typically entered.

iv

Discovery & motions

We obtain the State's evidence, depose witnesses, and challenge unlawful stops, searches, and statements.

v

Resolution or trial

Dismissal, diversion, a negotiated resolution, or a trial before a judge or jury.
