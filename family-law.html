Contested & Uncontested

Whether you agree on everything or nothing, you need the right plan.

Most family cases fall somewhere in between. We help you settle what can be settled efficiently, and we prepare to litigate what can't.

Uncontested

When you and the other party agree

An agreed divorce or parenting plan can save significant time, money, and conflict, but the paperwork still has to be right. A poorly drafted marital settlement agreement or parenting plan can cause problems for years.

  • Marital settlement agreements that cover property, debts, and support clearly
  • Parenting plans that anticipate holidays, school, travel, and communication
  • Child support worksheets that follow Florida's guidelines
  • Simplified or expedited filings where the law allows
Contested

When you can't agree

When the other side won't be reasonable, you need an attorney who prepares every case as though it will go to trial. That preparation is also what produces fair settlements.

  • Financial disclosure & discovery to uncover income and assets
  • Temporary relief hearings for support, timesharing, and use of the home
  • Mediation with a clear strategy and realistic goals
  • Evidentiary hearings & trial when settlement isn't possible
Family Law Services

Florida family-law matters we handle

From the first filing through post-judgment modifications and enforcement, we represent clients at every stage of a family case.

Fla. Stat. § 61.052

Divorce / Dissolution of Marriage

Florida is a no-fault state: a marriage can be dissolved when it is irretrievably broken. We handle contested and uncontested divorces, including cases involving businesses, retirement accounts, and significant debt.

Fla. Stat. § 61.13

Child Custody, Parenting Plans & Timesharing

Florida uses "parental responsibility" and "timesharing" instead of custody. We build parenting plans focused on your child's best interests and advocate for your relationship with your child.

Fla. Stat. § 61.30

Child Support

Support is calculated under Florida's guidelines based on both parents' incomes, the number of overnights, health insurance, and childcare. We make sure income is calculated accurately, including imputed and self-employment income.

Modification

Modification of Child Support

When income or a child's needs change substantially, support can be modified. We pursue and defend modifications and help ensure the new amount reflects current circumstances.

Modification

Modification of Parenting Plans & Timesharing

Children grow, schedules change, and sometimes a parenting plan stops working. A modification requires a substantial and material change in circumstances and a showing that the change is in the child's best interests.

Fla. Stat. Ch. 742

Paternity

When parents are not married, a father generally has no court-ordered timesharing rights until paternity is legally established. We handle paternity actions for fathers and mothers, along with the parenting plan and support that follow.

Enforcement · Contempt

Enforcement & Contempt Proceedings

A court order means little if the other party ignores it. We bring and defend motions for contempt and enforcement involving unpaid support, denied timesharing, and failures to follow a final judgment.

Fla. Stat. § 61.13001

Relocation

A parent who wants to move 50 miles or more from their principal residence for at least 60 consecutive days generally needs the other parent's written agreement or court approval. We represent parents seeking and opposing relocation.

Fla. Stat. § 61.08

Alimony

Florida's 2023 alimony reform eliminated permanent alimony. Courts may now award bridge-the-gap, rehabilitative, or durational alimony based on need, ability to pay, and the length of the marriage. We advise both paying and receiving spouses.

Fla. Stat. § 61.075

Property Division

Florida divides marital assets and debts through equitable distribution, starting from the premise of an equal split. We identify marital and non-marital property, trace assets, and address homes, businesses, and retirement accounts.

Fla. Stat. § 61.079

Prenuptial & Postnuptial Agreements

A well-drafted agreement protects both spouses and can prevent costly disputes later. We draft and review prenuptial and postnuptial agreements with full financial disclosure so they hold up when it matters.

How a Case Moves

The typical path of a Florida family case

Every case is different, but most follow the same general stages. We'll tell you where you are and what comes next at every step.

i

Consultation

We learn your goals, review your finances and parenting concerns, and explain your options and likely costs.

ii

Petition & response

The case is filed and served, or we respond to a petition filed against you, with requests for temporary relief if needed.

iii

Financial disclosure

Florida requires both parties to exchange financial affidavits and documents. This stage shapes support and property outcomes.

iv

Mediation

Most Florida family cases must attempt mediation before trial. Many settle here when both sides are well prepared.

v

Agreement or trial

An agreement is approved by the court, or the judge decides the contested issues after a hearing or trial.
