Florida divorce, handled with a clear plan.

To divorce in Florida, one spouse must have lived in the state for six months, and the court must find the marriage irretrievably broken. No one has to prove fault.

Contested vs. Uncontested

Two kinds of divorce, and most cases are a mix

An uncontested divorce means you and your spouse agree on everything: property, debts, support, and, if you have children, a parenting plan. The agreement is put in writing and approved by the court. It is faster and far less expensive, but the documents must be drafted carefully because they govern your life for years.

A contested divorce means at least one issue is disputed. The case moves through financial disclosure, discovery, mediation, and, if needed, a trial. Many contested cases settle once both sides have full financial information.

  • Simplified dissolution is available when both spouses agree on everything, there are no minor or dependent children, and neither seeks alimony
  • Marital settlement agreements resolve property, debt, and support in writing
  • Temporary relief can address support, timesharing, and who stays in the home while the case is pending
  • Your spouse cannot block a divorce by refusing to sign or participate

The Process

How a Florida divorce moves from filing to final judgment

A divorce begins when one spouse files a petition for dissolution of marriage in circuit court. In Orlando, family cases are heard in the Ninth Judicial Circuit, which covers Orange and Osceola counties.

How long it takes depends mostly on how much you and your spouse disagree. An uncontested case with complete paperwork can finish in weeks. A contested case can take many months or longer.

  • Petition and service. The petition is filed and served; the other spouse generally has 20 days to respond
  • Financial disclosure. Both spouses exchange financial affidavits and documents such as tax returns, pay stubs, and account statements
  • Parenting course. Parents of minor children must complete an approved parent education course
  • Mediation. Most contested family cases must attempt mediation before trial
  • Final hearing or trial. The judge approves your agreement or decides the disputed issues
