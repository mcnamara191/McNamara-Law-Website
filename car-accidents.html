Car Accident Injuries · Florida

Injured in a Florida car accident? Here's what happens next.

Florida's no-fault insurance system has short deadlines and rules that surprise most drivers. What you do in the first two weeks can affect your medical coverage and the value of your claim. After the Crash

Five steps to protect your health and your claim

i

Get medical care

Go to the ER, urgent care, or a doctor promptly, and within 14 days at the latest to preserve PIP benefits. Some injuries don't show symptoms right away.

ii

Report & document

Call law enforcement, get the report number, and photograph the vehicles, scene, and injuries. Collect witness contact information.

iii

Notify your insurer

Report the accident to your own insurance company promptly. Your PIP and possibly UM coverage come from your own policy.

iv

Be careful with adjusters

Don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer, and don't sign releases or accept a quick check before getting advice.

v

Talk to a lawyer

We identify every source of coverage, manage the claims, and protect your right to full compensation.

Medical Treatment

Your health comes first, and timing matters

Adrenaline can mask pain for hours or days after a crash. Neck and back injuries, concussions, and internal injuries are often missed when people "walk it off."

Under Florida's PIP law, you generally must receive initial medical services within 14 days of the accident for your PIP benefits to apply. And the full $10,000 in PIP medical benefits is generally available only if a qualified provider determines you had an emergency medical condition; otherwise, PIP medical benefits may be limited to $2,500.

  • Follow your treatment plan. Gaps in treatment are one of the first things insurers use to argue an injury wasn't serious.
  • Tell every provider about every symptom, including headaches, numbness, and trouble sleeping, so your records are complete.
  • Keep records of bills, prescriptions, mileage to appointments, and out-of-pocket costs.
  • Don't minimize your injuries on social media or to adjusters. Posts and casual comments are routinely used against injured people.
Insurance Claims

Several insurance claims, not just one

A single Florida car accident can involve several different insurance claims, often with different companies, each with its own rules, deadlines, and adjusters. Knowing which coverage applies, and in what order, is critical to getting fully compensated.

Insurance adjusters, including those from your own company, are trained to resolve claims for as little as possible. We handle the communication, gather the evidence, and push back when an offer doesn't reflect what you've lost.

  • PIP from your own policy, for initial medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault
  • Bodily injury liability from the at-fault driver's insurer, for damages beyond PIP
  • Uninsured / underinsured motorist from your own policy, when the at-fault driver has too little or no coverage
  • Property damage liability or your own collision coverage, for your vehicle
  • Commercial or rideshare policies when a business vehicle or Uber/Lyft driver is involved
  • Health insurance, MedPay, and other benefits that may fill gaps
PIP Benefits

Personal Injury Protection: Florida's no-fault coverage

Florida generally requires vehicle owners to carry $10,000 in Personal Injury Protection (PIP). PIP pays certain benefits after a crash regardless of who caused it, and it generally comes from your own policy, or a household member's, even if you were a passenger or a pedestrian.

Florida law changesThe Legislature has considered repealing or overhauling PIP in recent sessions. This page describes Florida's no-fault system as of its last update. We will confirm the rules that apply to the date of your accident.

What PIP Generally Covers

Up to $10,000 in combined benefits

  • 80% of reasonable medical expenses, subject to the 14-day and emergency-medical-condition rules
  • 60% of lost income and loss of earning capacity
  • Replacement services you can't perform because of your injuries
  • A $5,000 death benefit, in addition to the $10,000 limit

PIP does not cover pain and suffering, and $10,000 is often exhausted quickly. That's where bodily injury and UM claims come in.

Bodily Injury Claims

Holding the at-fault driver responsible

When your losses exceed what PIP pays, you can bring a bodily injury claim against the at-fault driver, typically paid by that driver's bodily injury liability insurance.

A surprising fact: Florida does not require most drivers to carry bodily injury liability coverage. Many drivers carry low limits or none at all. Identifying all available coverage early, including the driver's policy, a vehicle owner's policy, an employer's policy, and your own UM coverage, is one of the most important things we do.

  • Medical expenses not covered by PIP, including future treatment
  • Lost wages beyond the 60% PIP pays, and future lost earning capacity
  • Pain and suffering, mental anguish, and loss of enjoyment of life, when the injury meets Florida's threshold
  • Comparative fault can reduce a recovery, and being more than 50% at fault generally bars recovery from the other driver
Property Damage

Your vehicle, your rental, and your belongings

Florida requires vehicle owners to carry $10,000 in property damage liability coverage. You can usually pursue the at-fault driver's property damage coverage, or use your own collision coverage and let your insurer seek reimbursement.

Property damage disputes are common: lowball repair estimates, disagreements over whether a car is a total loss, unfair actual-cash-value offers, and rental car limits. If a body shop does poor work or overcharges, Florida's consumer protection laws may help. See repair-shop claims →

  • Repair costs or fair market value if the vehicle is totaled
  • Rental car or loss of use while your vehicle is out of service
  • Personal property damaged in the crash, such as phones, car seats, and equipment
  • Total-loss valuation disputes, using comparable vehicles and documentation
  • Photographs and estimates early, before repairs erase the evidence
Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist

When the at-fault driver has little or no insurance

Because bodily injury coverage isn't required for most Florida drivers, uninsured / underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage is often the most important coverage in a serious injury case. UM is part of your auto policy and steps in when the at-fault driver is uninsured, doesn't have enough coverage, or flees the scene.

Florida insurers must offer UM coverage, and it can only be declined in writing. Some policies are "stacked," multiplying coverage across vehicles. Even though it's your own policy, your insurer may still dispute the value of your claim, and we hold them to their obligations.

  • Hit-and-run crashes are typically covered by UM
  • Underinsured drivers with $10,000 or $25,000 policies may not come close to covering a serious injury
  • Stacked vs. non-stacked coverage can make a big difference in available limits
  • Household policies may provide coverage even if you weren't in your own vehicle
  • Check your declarations page, or let us review it for you
Lost Wages

Recovering the income you lost

PIP generally pays 60% of lost income, subject to the overall $10,000 limit. The remaining 40%, plus any income lost after PIP runs out, may be recoverable through a bodily injury or UM claim.

If your injuries affect your ability to work in the future, through reduced hours, a lower-paying role, or not being able to return to your job at all, you may also be able to recover loss of future earning capacity.

  • Pay stubs, W-2s, and tax returns establish what you were earning
  • A doctor's work restrictions connect time off to your injuries
  • Self-employed and gig workers can recover too, with the right documentation
  • Used PTO and sick days may be part of the claim
Pain & Suffering

Compensation for what an injury takes from your life

Pain and suffering, also called non-economic damages, compensates you for physical pain, mental anguish, inconvenience, and the loss of enjoyment of life. These damages are often the largest part of a serious injury claim, and they are not covered by PIP.

Under Florida's no-fault law, to recover pain and suffering from the at-fault driver in a car accident case, your injury generally must meet a threshold, such as a permanent injury within a reasonable degree of medical probability, significant and permanent scarring or disfigurement, or significant and permanent loss of an important bodily function. Thorough medical documentation is how that threshold is proven.

  • Consistent medical care creates the record needed to prove permanency
  • A journal of pain levels, limitations, and missed activities can be powerful evidence
  • Statements from family and coworkers show how your life has changed
  • Don't settle too early. Accepting an offer before you know the full extent of your injuries can leave you uncompensated for future care
Commercial Vehicles & Rideshare

Trucks, company vehicles, Uber, and Lyft

Commercial vehicle crashes involving semi-trucks, delivery vans, contractors' trucks, and company cars often involve multiple potentially responsible parties, including the driver, the employer, the vehicle owner, and maintenance companies, along with larger insurance policies. Critical evidence such as driver logs, electronic data, and dash-cam footage must be preserved quickly.

Rideshare crashes depend on the driver's app status. Florida law generally requires rideshare companies and drivers to carry significantly higher coverage, up to $1 million, once a ride has been accepted or a passenger is in the car, and lower coverage when the app is on but no ride has been accepted.

Rideshare Coverage Tiers

What was the driver doing?

  • App off: generally only the driver's personal auto policy applies
  • App on, waiting for a ride: Florida requires lower minimum coverage limits
  • Ride accepted or passenger on board: generally at least $1 million in coverage
  • Passengers, other drivers, pedestrians, and cyclists may all have claims
