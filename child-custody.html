How Florida Handles Custody

Florida no longer uses the word "custody"

Florida law divides what most people call custody into two parts. Parental responsibility is the authority to make major decisions about a child's education, health care, and upbringing. Courts usually order it shared between both parents. Timesharing is the schedule of when the child is with each parent.

Since July 2023, Florida law has started from a rebuttable presumption that equal timesharing is in a child's best interests. A parent who wants a different schedule must prove that equal time would not serve the child. The law gives no preference to mothers or fathers.

  • Shared parental responsibility is the norm; sole responsibility is ordered only when sharing would be detrimental to the child
  • Equal timesharing is the starting point, not a guarantee
  • Distance, work schedules, and school often determine what schedule is practical
  • Evidence of domestic violence, abuse, or neglect can change the outcome significantly
Best Interests of the Child

What judges look at

When parents cannot agree, the judge weighs a long list of statutory factors. No single factor decides a case. The court looks at the whole picture, which is why specific, documented evidence matters more than general accusations.

Statutory Factors Include

How a court evaluates each parent

  • Willingness to support the child's relationship with the other parent and honor the schedule
  • Each parent's involvement in school, medical care, and daily routines
  • Stability of each home and the value of continuity for the child
  • Each parent's ability to put the child's needs ahead of their own
  • The child's preference, if the child is mature enough to express one
  • Any history of domestic violence, abuse, neglect, or substance abuse
The Parenting Plan

A good plan prevents the next fight

Every Florida case involving timesharing requires a court-approved parenting plan. At a minimum it must describe how the parents will share daily responsibilities, the timesharing schedule, who is responsible for health care and school matters, and how the parents will communicate with the child.

Vague plans cause repeat litigation. We draft plans that answer the questions parents actually fight about later.

  • Weekly schedule, exchanges, and transportation
  • Holidays, school breaks, and summer
  • Travel, passports, and out-of-state trips
  • School choice and extracurricular activities
