Premises Liability Explained

What you must prove in a Florida slip-and-fall case

Falling on someone else's property doesn't automatically mean the owner is responsible. Florida law requires proof that the owner or business failed to use reasonable care.

For falls caused by a "transitory foreign substance" in a business, such as a spill, liquid, or dropped food, Florida Statute § 768.0755 requires the injured person to prove the business had actual or constructive knowledge of the dangerous condition and should have fixed it. Constructive knowledge can be shown by evidence that the condition existed long enough that the business should have discovered it, or that it occurred regularly and was foreseeable.

That evidence, including surveillance video, inspection logs, incident reports, and witness statements, can disappear quickly. Early investigation is often the difference between a strong claim and no claim.

After a Fall

Steps that protect your claim

  • Report it to the manager or owner, and ask for a copy of the incident report.
  • Photograph everything: the hazard, the area, lighting, warning signs or lack of them, and your injuries.
  • Get witness names and phone numbers before you leave.
  • Keep your shoes and clothing as they were at the time of the fall.
  • Get medical care promptly, and follow through with treatment.
  • Don't give a recorded statement to the property's insurer before talking with a lawyer.

For most negligence claims arising on or after March 24, 2023, Florida's statute of limitations is two years. Wrongful death claims are also generally subject to a two-year deadline. Claims against government entities have special notice requirements. Don't wait, because evidence disappears long before the deadline.

Florida uses modified comparative negligence. If you are 50% or less at fault, your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault. If you are found more than 50% at fault, you generally cannot recover damages from the other party.
